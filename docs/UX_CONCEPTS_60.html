Punkto PWA M8 — three first-visit UX concepts

Wireframe-level alternatives for the first-visit journey (blank device → a real place → its conversation). All three are evaluated against the same measured ground truth from CEO-OS #60 Phase A: the live publication holds 15 real records at ~10 distinct spatial addresses, 13 of which are CEO-OS acceptance-evidence records and only 2 are genuine user posts. The address unit is an exact xy_z triple, so "a place" is unforgiving — there is no such thing as "nearby" in the data model.

The concept

The publication is the interface. On first visit the reader lands on a plain list of published places — each line is one exact address with its thread folded in — and the map is demoted to a secondary "where is this" view. The three product ideas (addressability, distribution, persistence) are stated in one dismissible card, and the raw scope inputs hide behind Advanced.

  1. Land on the feed; the explainer states the three ideas in one line each.
  2. Tap Explore published places → the list of real places with their threads.
  3. Tap a place → its thread and reply composer, with the exact address in the header.
ForReads are 1 tap from anywhere. Zero protocol vocabulary on the first screen. Scales to a catalogue of places. No map interaction needed to read anything.
AgainstLoses the spatial feeling that is the product's differentiator. With only ~10 real addresses the list is thin and reads as a demo. The map becomes decoration.
Decision needed: is the product a place directory or a place you stand in? Concept A answers the first question, which may contradict the founder's stated lens ("the world is the interface").

The concept

The map stays the primary surface; the globe/3D orb is treated as the asset it is. A visitor is dropped onto a place that actually has content — not a generic starting area — and the thread is a sheet that rises from that exact point. The list becomes a searchable index ("places with text"), not the front door.

  1. Land on the map, centred on a real published place; each orb is a place with a thread.
  2. Tap the orb → the address sheet rises with the root post and its replies.
  3. Reply in place, or open the full thread; the exact address stays in the header.
ForBest match to the founder's lens. The spatial address becomes literally visible as a position. Places with no content are visually absent, which is honest.
AgainstWith ~10 places worldwide the "drop the newcomer on a place" move needs a curated start set — a product decision. On a slow phone, first map paint is a longer path to any content. A blank world is a real possibility and needs its own designed empty state.
Decision needed: which places get offered as the curated start set, and is "curated" acceptable given the founder's rule not to fabricate participants? (Proposal: curate only genuinely published addresses — the 10 real ones — and label them as operator-published rather than pretending they are community places.)

The concept

Reframe the object: a Punkto place is a conversation. The first screen is a list of open conversations with their reply counts and liveness. The spatial address is present but demoted to a verifiable line under each post — the map becomes the proof of place, not the navigation. The asymmetry is deliberate: reading is for humans who want words, and the spatial address is what makes the words non-reproducible.

  1. Land on open conversations with reply counts — the same real threads, now correctly grouped.
  2. Tap a conversation → full thread, exact address shown under the root post.
  3. Reply, or tap the address to open the map board sheet for the same place.
ForMaximises the chance a newcomer actually reads and replies — the network effect comes from threads, not from pins. The 15 QA records become obviously a backlog rather than looking like a community. Grouping replies is the whole product working.
AgainstWeakest expression of "the world is the interface"; spatial addressing becomes a footnote. With only 2 genuine posts the conversation list is nearly empty — the empty state carries the whole first impression and must be designed, not defaulted.
Decision needed: with 2 genuine public posts today, do we ship a conversation-first front door whose primary screen is nearly empty, or a place-directory front door (Concept A) that has 10 entries but 13 of which are our own QA records? Both are honest; they tell different stories about what Punkto is.
cross-cutting

What all three must answer: can a newcomer leave a mark without learning WGS84 ellipsoidal Z?

Measured answer today: NO. On the M8 branch, the create flow reaches the compose modal with an empty Punkti Z (ellipsoidal metres, WGS84) field, the location readout reads "Enter a known ellipsoidal height (metres, WGS84)", and publishing is blocked with "Enter a known WGS84 ellipsoidal height before publishing.". The height stage offers Ground and a height lever, but absolute ellipsoidal Z must still be typed by hand. A newcomer cannot leave a mark without understanding ellipsoidal Z.

Three ways out, all of which are founder decisions rather than engineering details:

  1. Derive it from the terrain. The map already knows the ground elevation at the chosen point, so Z can be prefilled from the terrain model and the newcomer only ever sees "floor" or "metres above ground". Honest, but it makes the published Z depend on a terrain source the node does not currently commit to.
  2. Make Z optional and honest. Publish with a declared uncertainty ("Z estimated from terrain" / "Z unknown") and let the address carry that fact. This changes the h encoding semantics — out of scope for this round, and a protocol-level decision.
  3. Keep it manual but explain it. One line of plain language ("height above the ellipsoid, like the altitude a flight tracker shows") plus a "use the ground here" default. Cheapest, keeps the protocol exact, still leaves the concept of Z in the newcomer path.

Recommendation to lock after founder review: option 3 now (plain-language wording + a terrain-ground default), option 1 as a follow-up once a terrain source is decided, option 2 never without a protocol change. None of the three is implemented in this PR — this is flagged for the founder rather than silently decided.
